Profit Boost vs Bonus Bet: A Key Difference

Both profit boosts and bonus bets are sportsbook promotions, but they work differently in one critical way: with a profit boost, your stake is returned on a win. You place real cash, and if your bet wins you get your money back plus the boosted profit. A bonus bet (free bet), by contrast, returns only the profit — the stake itself is gone regardless of outcome. The value of either offer depends on the complete terms, stake, prices and costs; the offer type alone does not establish a higher expected value.

How Boosted Odds Are Calculated

A profit boost increases the profit portion of your payout by a fixed percentage. A 50% boost on a +200 bet raises the effective odds to +300: the original +200 profit becomes +300 profit (200 × 1.5). A 25% boost on -110 odds raises the effective odds to approximately -88. The calculator handles all these conversions automatically — just enter the base odds and the boost percentage.

Why Hedge a Profit Boost?

A cash hedge can balance modeled outcomes when both wagers are accepted at the entered prices and settle under matching rules. The model is conditional: rejected or limited wagers, price changes, voids, fees and taxes can change the result.

When Is a Boost Worth Hedging?

Whether a boost is worth considering depends on both prices, accepted stakes, caps, costs and settlement rules. Enter the complete offer in the calculator and compare both modeled outcomes. No boost percentage alone guarantees a positive return.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a profit boost?
A profit boost is a sportsbook promotion that enhances the payout on a selected bet by a specified percentage. Your original stake is returned on a win, subject to the offer’s restrictions and caps.
How is a profit boost different from a free bet?
With a free bet you only collect the profit on a win — your stake is forfeited. With a profit boost you place your own cash and get it back plus boosted winnings if you win. The offer type alone does not determine a conversion rate; compare the full inputs.
Should I always hedge a profit boost?
No. The percentage alone is insufficient. Compare both prices, caps, accepted stakes, costs and settlement rules before deciding.
