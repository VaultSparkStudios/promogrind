Sports Betting Arbitrage Explained — How to Model Hedged Returns

Arbitrage is a pricing condition in which covering every outcome can produce a positive modeled return. Actual returns depend on accepting and settling every leg at the prices used in the calculation.

Identify the pricing condition

For two mutually exclusive outcomes that cover the whole market, add the inverse of each decimal price. A sum below 1 indicates a positive return in the model.

Implied probability sum = 1 / decimal odds A + 1 / decimal odds B

Hypothetical example

Two outcomes priced at +105 (2.05 decimal)
Combined implied probability2 ÷ 2.05 = 97.56%
Probability margin100% − 97.56% = 2.44%
Total stake$1,000: $500 on each outcome
Payout for either modeled outcome$500 × 2.05 = $1,025
Net before costs$1,025 − $1,000 = $25
Return on total stake$25 ÷ $1,000 = 2.50%

The probability margin and return on stake are different measures. This illustration assumes exactly two outcomes, identical settlement rules and full acceptance of both $500 stakes. It is not a current offer.

Split unequal prices

For unequal decimal prices, use Stake A = Total stake × (1 / price A) ÷ (1 / price A + 1 / price B). Apply the same formula to B. The 2-Way Arbitrage Calculator shows each stake and modeled payout.

Compare the same market

Check event, market, start time, overtime rules and void conditions. A market with a draw needs all three outcomes covered; use the 3-Way Calculator where appropriate.

Execution risks

  • One leg may be rejected or limited after another is accepted.
  • Prices may change before both stakes are placed.
  • Settlement and void rules may differ between operators.
  • Rounding, fees, tax and cash requirements affect the result.

A calculator does not remove these risks. Follow operator terms and applicable rules; do not place extra wagers to conceal your activity.
