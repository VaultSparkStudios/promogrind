Report winnings even without a tax form

Gambling winnings generally belong on your federal income tax return even when no Form W-2G arrives. A sportsbook's reporting threshold is not a tax-free allowance. See IRS Topic 419.

What changed for 2026

The IRS lists a $2,000 minimum reporting threshold for relevant payments made in 2026. For sports wagering, the W-2G reporting rule also considers whether winnings are at least 300 times the wager. Withholding rules are separate. Consult the current W-2G instructions for the complete requirements.

For tax years beginning after 2025, the federal wagering-loss deduction is limited to 90% of qualifying losses, capped by wagering gains. Recreational gamblers generally must itemize to claim it. Breaking even on bets can therefore still leave taxable gambling income. The IRS 2026-19 bulletin explains the changed limit.

Keep the underlying records

  • Dates, sportsbooks and transaction references.
  • Wagers, payouts, voids, cash-out adjustments and losses.
  • Bonus terms, account statements, tax forms and any tax withheld.

Review state rules and your classification

State treatment can differ from federal treatment. Professional-gambler classification, promotional credits and expense treatment also require individual review. Ask your tax professional to check the rules for your location and tax year rather than applying a single percentage to your net app profit.
